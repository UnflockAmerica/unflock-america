# UNFLOCK AMERICA — CAMPAIGN PLAYBOOK
## Cancel Your City's Flock Contract in 90 Days

**WARNING:** This playbook requires you to keep your identity and activities separate from any personal or operational infrastructure that could be traced back to you. Use public libraries for research, use a VPN, communicate through encrypted channels. Justice doesn't need a name, and OPSEC ensures you survive to fight another day.

---

## THE THEORY OF VICTORY

Flock Safety makes money by selling annual contracts to cities, HOAs, and police departments. Each contract is a revenue stream. Every time a city cancels, that stream dries up — and the political signal grows.

**We don't need to win a Supreme Court case to win this fight.** We just need to make Flock contracts politically toxic. Every city council that votes to renew a Flock contract needs to know they're voting to:

1. Enable warrantless mass surveillance
2. Track women seeking reproductive healthcare
3. Enable racial profiling of minority communities
4. Track peaceful protesters
5. Share your constituents' data with federal agencies without oversight

When the cost of renewing becomes higher than the cost of canceling — we win. And 30+ cities have already proven it works.

## PHASE 1: RECONNAISSANCE (Days 1-14)

### Week 1: Know Your Battlefield

**Day 1-3: Open Source Intelligence**

- [ ] **Find your city's Flock contract.** Search for: `[city name] Flock Safety contract`, `[city name] ALPR agreement`, `[city name] police department license plate reader`
- [ ] **Search public records.** Check city council agendas for the last 12 months — Flock renewals, ALPR purchases, or surveillance technology approvals will be listed
- [ ] **Check the city budget.** Line items for "ALPR," "license plate readers," "Flock Safety" — and how much they're paying
- [ ] **Map the cameras.** Drive around. Note locations of the distinctive white Flock cameras (they're mounted on poles, usually at intersections). Check Google Maps street view for camera locations.

**Day 4-7: Identify Decision Timeline**

- [ ] **Find the contract end date.** Most Flock contracts are 1-3 years with auto-renewal clauses. Find out when yours expires.
- [ ] **Check for auto-renewal.** Many contracts renew automatically. You need to act before the auto-renewal notice period (often 30-60 days before expiration).
- [ ] **Identify the decision maker.** Who can cancel the contract? (City council vote? Police chief signature? City manager?) Find out and note the process.
- [ ] **Identify the Flock point person.** There's usually a police department liaison or IT person who manages the Flock relationship. Their name is in the contract.

### Week 2: Gather Evidence

**Day 8-10: File FOIA** — Use the FOIA template from this kit.

- [ ] File your public records request with the police department
- [ ] CC the city clerk
- [ ] Set a calendar reminder for the statutory response deadline
- [ ] If they deny or stall: file an appeal with the state Public Access Counselor or Attorney General

**Day 11-14: Build Your Evidence File**

- [ ] Compile all known Flock abuse incidents (national — from EFF, ACLU, Institute for Justice databases)
- [ ] Compile local evidence from the FOIA response
- [ ] Create a one-page summary sheet: "What Flock Is Doing in [Your City]"
- [ ] Create a list of every city council member, their contact info, and their known positions on privacy/surveillance

## PHASE 2: BUILD THE COALITION (Days 15-30)

### Week 3: Recruit

**American civil liberties coalition:**

- [ ] **Contact your local ACLU affiliate** — they already have a "Get the Flock Out" campaign. They have attorneys, organizers, and press contacts
- [ ] **Contact EFF** — they're litigating against Flock and can provide expert testimony
- [ ] **Contact your local NAACP chapter** — racial profiling via Flock is an established issue
- [ ] **Contact Planned Parenthood / reproductive rights organizations** — the Texas abortion surveillance case is a powerful motivator
- [ ] **Contact Indivisible / Swing Left / local progressive groups** — protest surveillance resonates
- [ ] **Contact local libertarian groups** — this is the rare issue where the ACLU and the Cato Institute agree
- [ ] **Contact the local teachers union / labor unions** — if you can show Flock data being shared with ICE

**One person from each group becomes your Steering Committee.** Meet weekly via Signal.

### Week 4: Message Development

- [ ] **Craft your local narrative.** What Flock abuse is most resonant in your community? (Abortion surveillance? Racial targeting? Protest tracking?)
- [ ] **Find your plaintiff.** Is there a local person whose Flock data was misused? A protester who was tracked? Someone who received a knock on the door based on their driving patterns?
- [ ] **Create a name for your local campaign.** "UnFlock [City Name]" or "Know Your Rights [City]" — something local, sticky, and action-oriented
- [ ] **Create a one-page fact sheet** — specific to your city: number of cameras, contract amount, expiration date, council member positions
- [ ] **Set up a Signal group** for the steering committee
- [ ] **Set up a ProtonMail** for press contacts
- [ ] **Create a public presence** — a simple Carrd or GitHub Pages site that states the problem and the demand

## PHASE 3: THE PRESSURE CAMPAIGN (Days 31-60)

### Week 5: Public Education

- [ ] **Op-ed in the local paper.** Pitch your coalition's leader to write an op-ed: "Why [City] Should Cancel Its Flock Contract"
- [ ] **Local press outreach.** Contact reporters who cover city council, policing, or technology. NPR's local affiliate, the local paper, local TV news. Give them the FOIA data you got back
- [ ] **Social media campaign.** Daily posts with facts from the national and local evidence. Tag city council members
- [ ] **Community meeting.** Host a public meeting at a library or community center. Present the evidence. Answer questions. Sign people up to speak at city council
- [ ] **Petition.** Start a public petition (change.org or local version) demanding the city council put Flock on the agenda for public hearing

### Week 6: Council Relations

- [ ] **Meet with every council member individually.** Start with the privacy-friendly ones. End with the skeptics.
  - For allies: ask them to sponsor the ordinance
  - For undecided: give them the evidence file
  - For opponents: understand their objections (usually "crime prevention" — have the rebuttal ready)
- [ ] **Identify your swing vote.** There's always one council member who could go either way. Concentrate your effort there
- [ ] **Letter-writing campaign.** Ask supporters to write personalized letters to THEIR council member. Do not use form letters — they are ignored
- [ ] **Prepare the model ordinance** for introduction. Work with a friendly council member or the city attorney to adapt it to local law

### Week 7: Research the Opposition

- [ ] **Request Flock's response.** Flock will send representatives to council meetings. They have a standard playbook:
  - "We only retain data for 7 days" (╳ — true but irrelevant, since abuse happens within those 7 days)
  - "Data is only shared when agencies opt in" (╳ — defaults often enable wide sharing)
  - "We have safeguards against misuse" (╳ — proven false, tests with "hehehe" as search reason passed)
  - "This technology solves crimes" (╳ — ask for local data on crimes solved via Flock vs. clearance rates before/after)
  - "Without Flock, you're letting criminals go free" (╳ — fear-based, not evidence-based)
- [ ] **Prepare rebuttals** to every Flock talking point. Anticipate what they'll say to each council member

## PHASE 4: THE VOTE (Days 61-90)

### Week 8: Public Hearing Preparation

- [ ] **Pack the room.** Coordinate speakers for council meeting. Use the testimony template from this kit
- [ ] **Diverse speaker lineup.** Get: civil liberties lawyer, person of color affected by racial profiling, woman concerned about reproductive privacy, religious leader, retired police officer (if you can find one who opposes Flock)
- [ ] **Visuals.** Large printouts showing the number of warrantless searches, the map of cameras in the city, the national abuse map
- [ ] **Press advisory.** Send to media 48 hours before the hearing. "City Council to Vote on Flock Safety Contract — Coalition of [X] Organizations to Testify"
- [ ] **Live-stream** the hearing if the city doesn't already

### Week 9: The Hearing

- [ ] **Arrive early.** Fill the chamber. Supporters should wear the same color (blue or black) to show unity visually
- [ ] **Deliver testimony.** 2-3 minutes each. Stick to the script. No grandstanding. Facts, not emotion (even though we feel the emotion)
- [ ] **Have the ordinance text ready** for introduction
- [ ] **Record everything** — audio and video. Disputes from council members become future evidence
- [ ] **Have a plan B.** If the vote is delayed, amend the request to at minimum: (a) pause any expansion of cameras, (b) require an audit, (c) require a warrant for all searches pending the next vote

### Week 10: Close the Deal

- [ ] **Vote day.** Coordinate a final push — calls, emails, social media. Make it impossible for council members to vote "no" without public explanation
- [ ] **Celebrate the win.** If you win: press release, social media, thank the coalition. The win becomes the template for the next city
- [ ] **Fight the appeal.** If you lose: Flock and the PD may try to ignore the ordinance. Be ready with a lawsuit threat. File a complaint with the state AG

## WHAT TO DO WITH EACH OUTCOME

### If you WIN (ordinance passes, contract canceled):

1. **Document everything** you did. Write a post-mortem. Every tactic, every failure, every breakthrough
2. **Share it.** Publish the playbook with your city's specific lessons. The next city needs to not start from scratch
3. **Verify compliance.** The ordinance requires Flock to destroy data within the retention period. FOIA for proof of destruction
4. **Pass the next ordinance.** Expand the protections. Ban ALPR expansion. Require audits of any other surveillance tech

### If you LOSE (contract renewed, ordinance defeated):

1. **Know why.** Who voted no? Why? Was it a single council member? A procedural trick? Lack of public pressure?
2. **Publish the votes.** Publicly shame the no-votes — but factually. "Councilmember X voted to allow warrantless tracking of your family." Let the voters judge
3. **File a lawsuit.** If the city violated its own procurement rules, state law, or the state constitution — sue. Bring it to the ACLU/EFF
4. **Go to the ballot.** If permitted in your jurisdiction, put a surveillance ordinance on the ballot as a ballot measure. Let the people decide

## OPSEC RULES

1. **Create a separate identity** for this work. ProtonMail. Signal. No connection to your personal life.
2. **Never log into campaign accounts** from your home network. Use public wifi, a VPN, or a library computer.
3. **Never post from your personal social media.** Create new accounts. Their metadata is separate.
4. **Communicate with coalition members** via encrypted channels only (Signal recommended).
5. **Meet in person** for sensitive strategy sessions. Coffee shops. Public libraries. Not recorded.
6. **Assume Flock monitors opposition.** They are a surveillance company. They have PR and legal teams watching social media, city council recordings, and news coverage.
7. **Never share evidence sources** that could identify your FOIA requesters. Redact everything.
8. **Burner phone** if you're doing in-person field work (mapping cameras, talking to community members). Cheap prepaid. Cash purchase.
