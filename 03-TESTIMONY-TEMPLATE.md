## Citizen Script for City Council Meetings

**USE THIS WHEN:** Your city council is considering renewing a Flock contract, expanding ALPR cameras, or hearing a surveillance technology ordinance.

**TIME LIMIT:** Most councils give 2-3 minutes per speaker. Read slowly. This script is timed at approximately 2 minutes and 30 seconds at a measured speaking pace.

### SCRIPT

**Madam/Mister Mayor, members of the [City Council / Board of Supervisors]:**

My name is **[Your Full Name]**. I'm a resident of **[Your Neighborhood/Ward/District]** , and I'm here tonight to speak about Flock Safety — specifically, I'm here to ask you not to renew our contract with them.

I'm not a lawyer. I'm not a politician. I'm a citizen who read the Fourth Amendment — and I think Flock Safety violates it, every day, on our streets.

**Here's what Flock does:** Every time I drive past one of their cameras — going to work, picking up my kids, visiting my doctor — it records my license plate, my location, the exact time. That data goes into a national database. My movements are stored. My patterns are learned. And all of this happens without a single warrant.

The Supreme Court said in *Carpenter v. United States* that the government can't track you over time without a warrant. Flock Safety lets the government do exactly that — 83,000 cameras across the country, and right here in **[Your City]** .

**Now here's what's happened with Flock around the country — not hypothetical, not speculation — it happened:**

In Texas, police used Flock data to track a woman seeking reproductive healthcare. They pretended it was a missing person case. They ran a single search across 83,000 cameras — across nearly the entire country — to surveil a woman for exercising a constitutional right.

Police in multiple states have used Flock's "Convoy" feature to target Romani communities with racial and ethnic slurs as search terms. Not an investigation. Discrimination.

During the 2025 protests, Flock data was used to track Americans exercising their First Amendment rights.

In San Jose, police searched their Flock database nearly **four million times** in a single year without warrants.

In Los Altos, Flock data was shared with out-of-state agencies more than **one million times**.

The California Attorney General is now suing a city for the exact kind of data sharing Flock enabled.

**And Flock knew.** They claimed they had safeguards. They claimed you couldn't search for abortion-related reasons. But police officers just typed "hehehe" as the search reason — and it went through. They switched to a drop-down menu, and officers just picked whatever reason they wanted, true or not. The safeguards were theater.

**So here's what I'm asking of you tonight:**

First — don't renew the Flock contract. At minimum, pause it and hold a public hearing where we can see the evidence.

Second — pass an ordinance that requires a warrant for any ALPR search in our city. Data retention limited to 7 days. An annual public audit that shows us who searched what and why. And a prohibition on sharing our data with out-of-state agencies — including federal agencies — without an actual warrant.

Over thirty cities have already done this — Austin, Cambridge, Eugene, Santa Cruz, Flagstaff. They canceled their contracts. Their crime-solving didn't collapse. But their residents' privacy was restored.

**[Councilmember Name],** I'm asking you directly: will you vote to renew a contract with a company that enabled the surveillance of women seeking healthcare, the targeting of ethnic minorities, and the tracking of peaceful protesters? Or will you stand with the Constitution?

**If you want to see the evidence,** I've brought copies of the EFF investigation, the ACLU reports, and an Institute for Justice database documenting over 100 incidents of ALPR abuse. I'm leaving them with the clerk.

Thank you for your time. I'm happy to answer any questions.
