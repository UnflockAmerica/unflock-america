# MODEL MUNICIPAL ORDINANCE
## Restricting Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) Surveillance

**TITLE:** An Ordinance Restricting the Acquisition, Use, Retention, and Sharing of Automated License Plate Reader Data by the [CITY/COUNTY NAME] Police Department

**BE IT ORDAINED** by the [City Council / Board of Supervisors] of [City/County Name]:

---

### SECTION 1: FINDINGS AND PURPOSE

The [Council/Board] finds and declares:

**(a)** The Fourth Amendment to the United States Constitution protects the right of the people to be secure against unreasonable searches and seizures. In *Carpenter v. United States*, 585 U.S. ___ (2018), the Supreme Court held that warrantless acquisition of long-term cell-site location records constitutes a search under the Fourth Amendment because it allows the government to track an individual's "movements over an extended period" and "piec[e] together a detailed, comprehensive picture of one's public movements."

**(b)** Automated License Plate Reader ("ALPR") systems, including but not limited to those operated by Flock Safety and its successors or affiliates, capture and record the license plate, date, time, and GPS location of every vehicle passing within their field of view. A network of such cameras — including the over 83,000 cameras currently operated by Flock Safety alone — enables warrantless tracking of individuals' comings and goings across multiple jurisdictions, revealing political associations, reproductive healthcare decisions, religious attendance, medical visits, and other constitutionally protected activity.

**(c)** Investigations by the Electronic Frontier Foundation, the American Civil Liberties Union, the Institute for Justice, and multiple news organizations have documented over 100 separate incidents of ALPR abuse nationwide, including:

1. Use of Flock data to track protesters exercising First Amendment rights during the 2025 "No Kings" protests;
2. Discriminatory searches using racial and ethnic slurs to target Romani communities and other minority groups;
3. Use of Flock data to surveil women seeking reproductive healthcare, including a documented case in Johnson County, Texas, where a single query accessed 83,345 cameras across nearly the entire country;
4. Over 3.9 million warrantless searches of San Jose, California's ALPR database in a single 12-month period;
5. Over 1.6 million instances of federal agency access to San Francisco Police Department Flock data;
6. Systematic sharing of local ALPR data with out-of-state law enforcement agencies without probable cause, warrant, or oversight.

**(d)** In 2025-2026, more than thirty (30) cities and municipalities across the United States — including Austin, TX; Eugene, OR; Cambridge, MA; Santa Cruz, CA; Flagstaff, AZ; and Evanston, IL — have canceled or refused to renew Flock Safety contracts following public organizing campaigns.

**(e)** The State of Washington enacted SB 6002 in 2026, establishing that evidence obtained in knowing violation of ALPR regulations is inadmissible in court and imposing civil and criminal penalties for unauthorized access or dissemination of ALPR data.

**(f)** The purpose of this Ordinance is to protect the constitutional rights of [City/County] residents, to establish uniform standards for the acquisition and use of ALPR technology by the [City/County] Police Department, to prevent the use of municipal surveillance resources for out-of-state law enforcement activities lacking independent constitutional justification, and to ensure meaningful public oversight of surveillance technology.

---

### SECTION 2: DEFINITIONS

**(a) "ALPR System"** means any device, software, or network of devices capable of automatically capturing and recording license plate numbers, dates, times, and/or geographic locations of vehicles, including but not limited to fixed cameras, mobile cameras, and cloud-based or networked systems such as Flock Safety, Vigilant Solutions/Motorola Solutions, and any substantially similar technology.

**(b) "ALPR Data"** means any information captured, recorded, stored, processed, or transmitted by an ALPR System, including license plate numbers, vehicle descriptors (make, model, color), date and time stamps, GPS coordinates, camera location identifiers, and any metadata or analysis derived therefrom, including pattern-of-life analysis, convoy identification, or association mapping.

**(c) "Authorized Purpose"** means:

1. Investigation of a specific, identified crime that has already occurred, where the ALPR data sought is directly relevant to that investigation and probable cause otherwise exists; or
2. A BOLO (Be On the Lookout) alert for a specific vehicle associated with an identified missing person in imminent danger, an Amber Alert, or a Blue Alert.

Provided that "Authorized Purpose" expressly **excludes** (i) general crime prevention, (ii) immigration enforcement, (iii) traffic enforcement not relating to the specific crimes described above, (iv) civil regulatory enforcement, (v) reproductive healthcare investigations, (vi) First Amendment-protected activity monitoring, and (vii) any purpose not specifically enumerated in this subsection.

**(d) "Independent Probable Cause"** means specific, articulable facts that would lead a reasonable person to believe that a particular crime has been committed and that the ALPR data sought will produce evidence of that crime, as determined by a neutral and detached judicial officer issuing a warrant.

**(e) "Out-of-State Law Enforcement Agency"** means any federal, state, county, municipal, or tribal law enforcement agency, or any subunit thereof, whose primary jurisdiction lies outside the boundaries of [State Name].

**(f) "Authorized User"** means an employee of the [City/County] Police Department who has (i) successfully completed annual training on the constitutional requirements of this Ordinance, (ii) been individually certified by the Chief of Police or designee, and (iii) been registered in the Department's ALPR audit log.

---

### SECTION 3: WARRANT REQUIREMENT

**(a)** No [City/County] Police Department employee, agent, or contractor shall access, search, query, or otherwise use any ALPR Data for any purpose absent a warrant issued by a neutral and detached judicial officer based on independent probable cause, except as provided in Section 3(b).

**(b) Exigent Circumstances Exception.** Absent a warrant, ALPR Data may be searched only where:

1. The authorized user has an objectively reasonable belief that an identified person is in immediate danger of death or serious bodily injury; and
2. The search is strictly limited to data sufficient to locate the person; and
3. A written record of the search, including the factual basis for the exigency, is filed with the Chief of Police within 72 hours; and
4. A warrant application is filed within 72 hours of the exigent search. If the warrant is denied, all evidence derived from the exigent search shall be suppressed and destroyed.

**(c)** No ALPR Data shall be searched using criteria designed to identify or track individuals based on their (i) race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, gender, sexual orientation, or political affiliation; (ii) attendance at political protests, religious services, reproductive healthcare facilities, or other First Amendment-protected activities; or (iii) association with any person, group, or organization based on the criteria listed in (i) or (ii) above.

**(d)** The "Convoy," "pattern-matching," "association mapping," or any substantially similar feature that identifies vehicles traveling together or infers social associations between vehicle owners is expressly **prohibited** from use against any person within the jurisdiction of [City/County].

---

### SECTION 4: DATA RETENTION AND DESTRUCTION

**(a)** ALPR Data captured by [City/County] ALPR Systems shall be retained for a maximum of **seven (7) calendar days** from the date of capture.

**(b)** ALPR Data shall be permanently and irreversibly destroyed no later than the eighth (8th) calendar day following capture, unless the data is subject to an active preservation order issued by a court of competent jurisdiction in connection with a pending criminal investigation for which a warrant has been obtained under Section 3.

**(c)** In the event that ALPR Data is stored or processed on a vendor's cloud infrastructure (including but not limited to Flock Safety's cloud platform), the [City/County] shall require by contract that the vendor:

1. Enforce identical retention and destruction timelines;
2. Provide written certification of data destruction within 10 business days;
3. Prohibit the vendor from retaining, accessing, using, selling, or sharing [City/County] ALPR Data for any purpose whatsoever, including product improvement, analytics, or anonymized aggregation;
4. Subject vendor records to independent annual audit at vendor expense.

**(d)** Any personally identifiable information or vehicle descriptor data retained beyond the period authorized by this Section shall be inadmissible in any judicial or administrative proceeding, and the responsible custodian shall be subject to discipline, up to and including termination.

---

### SECTION 5: PROHIBITION ON OUT-OF-STATE DATA SHARING

**(a)** No ALPR Data captured by [City/County] ALPR Systems shall be shared, transmitted, sold, or otherwise made accessible to any out-of-state law enforcement agency, any federal law enforcement agency, or any third party not specifically authorized by this Ordinance.

**(b)** ALPR Data stored in vendor-operated cloud infrastructure shall be geographically restricted to servers located within the State of [State Name].

**(c)** Any vendor contract for ALPR systems or services shall include a contractual provision expressly prohibiting the vendor from (i) sharing [City/County] ALPR Data with any law enforcement agency outside [State Name]; (ii) participating in any multi-jurisdictional data-sharing network that includes out-of-state or federal agencies; or (iii) granting access to [City/County] ALPR Data through any feature, setting, or default configuration that may be activated by any entity other than the [City/County] Police Department Chief or their written designee.

**(d)** Violation of this Section by a vendor shall constitute a material breach of contract and shall result in immediate termination of the contract and permanent debarment of the vendor from future contracts with [City/County].

---

### SECTION 6: ANNUAL PUBLIC AUDIT AND TRANSPARENCY

**(a)** The [City/County] Police Department shall publish an annual audit of all ALPR System usage, including:

1. Total number of warrant-authorized searches during the audit period;
2. Total number of exigent circumstance searches during the audit period, including a narrative summary of each exigent search sufficient to assess its constitutionality;
3. Total number of ALPR cameras operated within [City/County] boundaries, with their general locations (by census tract, not exact coordinates);
4. Total number of license plate captures during the audit period;
5. Number and description of any data-sharing requests received from out-of-state or federal agencies, and the disposition of each;
6. Number of instances in which vendor defaults, settings, or configurations caused data to be shared beyond the scope authorized by this Ordinance;
7. Any complaints received regarding ALPR misuse, and their disposition.

**(b)** The annual audit shall be published on the [City/County] website in a machine-readable, downloadable format and presented at a public meeting of the [City Council/Board of Supervisors].

**(c)** The audit shall be conducted by an independent third-party auditor selected by the [City Council/Board of Supervisors], not by the Police Department.

---

### SECTION 7: CONTRACT REQUIREMENTS

**(a)** No ALPR system or surveillance technology contract may be entered into, renewed, or extended by the [City/County] unless:

1. The contract has been reviewed by the [City/County] Attorney for compliance with this Ordinance and all applicable constitutional requirements;
2. A draft of the contract has been published on the [City/County] website for public comment for a minimum of thirty (30) calendar days;
3. A public hearing has been held before the [City Council/Board of Supervisors] at least fourteen (14) calendar days following the close of the public comment period;
4. The [City Council/Board of Supervisors] has voted to approve the contract by an affirmative majority vote recorded in open session.

**(b)** Any ALPR contract entered into without compliance with this Section is void ab initio.

**(c)** This Section applies to all renewals and extensions of existing contracts, including automatic renewals.

---

### SECTION 8: PRIVATE RIGHT OF ACTION

**(a)** Any person whose ALPR data was accessed, used, shared, or retained in violation of this Ordinance may bring a civil action against [City/County], any law enforcement officer or employee who knowingly violated this Ordinance, and/or any vendor that knowingly facilitated such violation.

**(b)** In any such action, the prevailing plaintiff shall be entitled to recover:

1. Actual damages, or liquidated damages of $2,500 per violation, whichever is greater;
2. Punitive damages for willful, wanton, or reckless violations;
3. Reasonable attorney's fees and costs;
4. Injunctive relief ordering the destruction of unlawfully retained data and requiring compliance with this Ordinance.

**(c)** Sovereign immunity is waived to the fullest extent permitted by law for claims arising under this Section.

---

### SECTION 9: EVIDENCE SUPPRESSION

**(a)** Any ALPR Data obtained, accessed, or used in violation of this Ordinance, and any evidence derived therefrom, shall be inadmissible in any criminal, civil, administrative, or other proceeding in [City/County].

**(b)** The [City/County] Police Department shall maintain a chain-of-custody log for all ALPR Data accessed for investigatory purposes, identifying the authorized user, the warrant or exigent circumstance authorization, the date/time of access, and the search criteria used.

---

### SECTION 10: VENDOR OVERSIGHT AND ACCOUNTABILITY

**(a)** The [City/County] shall maintain a publicly accessible register of all vendors providing ALPR or surveillance technology services to the [City/County] Police Department, including contract terms, dollar amounts, and termination dates.

**(b)** Any vendor that provides ALPR services to [City/County] must, at its sole expense, submit to an independent security and privacy audit conducted by a firm selected by the [City Council/Board of Supervisors]. The audit results shall be published in full.

**(c)** Any vendor found to have made materially false statements regarding data retention, data sharing, search controls, or privacy protections shall have its contract immediately terminated and be permanently debarred from future contracting with [City/County].

---

### SECTION 11: SEVERABILITY

If any provision, clause, sentence, or phrase of this Ordinance is for any reason held to be invalid or unconstitutional by a court of competent jurisdiction, such decision shall not affect the validity of the remaining portions of this Ordinance. The [City Council/Board of Supervisors] hereby declares that it would have passed this Ordinance and each provision thereof irrespective of the fact that any one or more provisions may be declared invalid or unconstitutional.

---

### SECTION 12: EFFECTIVE DATE

This Ordinance shall take effect thirty (30) calendar days following its adoption by the [City Council/Board of Supervisors].

---

### SECTION 13: CONFLICT WITH OTHER LAWS

To the extent that any existing ordinance, resolution, policy, or contract conflicts with the provisions of this Ordinance, the more restrictive provision shall control.
