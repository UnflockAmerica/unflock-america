### REQUESTED RECORDS

**1. ALPR Camera Inventory**

1.1. The total number of Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) cameras currently operated, owned, leased, or maintained by or on behalf of your agency, including fixed cameras, mobile cameras (vehicle-mounted), and any cameras operated by a third-party vendor (e.g., Flock Safety, Vigilant Solutions/Motorola Solutions) within your jurisdiction.

1.2. For each camera: (a) its physical location by address or intersection; (b) the date of installation; (c) the vendor name and contract number; (d) whether it is a fixed or mobile unit; and (e) the current operational status.

**2. Contracts and Agreements**

2.1. All contracts, memoranda of understanding, service agreements, license agreements, or any other agreements between your agency and Flock Safety, and/or any ALPR vendor, including all amendments, addenda, exhibits, and attachments.

2.2. All invoices, purchase orders, and payment records related to ALPR systems, including initial purchase/lease costs and recurring monthly/annual fees.

2.3. Any data-sharing agreements, memoranda of understanding, or other arrangements permitting other law enforcement agencies (local, state, federal, or out-of-state) to access or search your agency's ALPR data.

**3. Search and Access Logs**

3.1. A complete log of all searches, queries, or accesses of ALPR data conducted by your agency or by any individual on behalf of your agency, from [DATE - at least 12 months prior] to present, including for each search:

(a) Date and time of search;
(b) Username or employee identifier of the searcher;
(c) Search criteria used (e.g., license plate number, vehicle description, date range, location radius);
(d) Purpose or reason for the search as recorded in the system;
(e) Whether a warrant or exigent circumstances exception was documented;
(f) Number of records returned;
(g) Whether any data was exported, downloaded, or shared externally, and with whom.

3.2. A complete log of all instances in which ALPR data captured by your agency's cameras was accessed by any out-of-state law enforcement agency, federal law enforcement agency, or any non-law-enforcement third party.

**4. Training Materials and Policies**

4.1. All current policies, procedures, directives, general orders, or standard operating procedures governing the use, access, retention, sharing, and destruction of ALPR data.

4.2. All training materials, presentations, manuals, or guides provided to your agency's personnel regarding the use of ALPR systems, including training on privacy, constitutional requirements, and data handling.

4.3. Records of which employees have completed ALPR training, including dates of completion.

**5. Data Retention and Destruction**

5.1. Records documenting the current data retention period(s) for ALPR data in your agency's systems.

5.2. Records of any data destruction logs, confirming that ALPR data has been destroyed in accordance with retention policies.

5.3. Any communications from ALPR vendors regarding default data retention settings, including any settings that may cause data to be retained longer than the stated policy or shared beyond your agency's authorized users.

**6. Misuse and Complaints**

6.1. Any records of complaints, internal affairs investigations, or disciplinary actions related to ALPR system misuse, unauthorized access, or privacy violations.

6.2. Any legal notices, demand letters, or lawsuits filed against your agency related to ALPR data, including but not limited to privacy claims, civil rights claims, or constitutional claims.

**7. Vendor Security and Privacy**

7.1. All privacy impact assessments, security audits, vulnerability assessments, or penetration test results related to any ALPR system used by your agency.

7.2. Any records of data breaches, unauthorized access incidents, or security incidents involving ALPR data or ALPR systems used by your agency.

7.3. Any documentation provided by the vendor regarding: (a) where ALPR data is stored (geographic location of servers); (b) who has access to the data (vendor employees, subcontractors, third parties); (c) whether the data is used for vendor product improvement, machine learning training, or any purpose other than law enforcement; and (d) whether the data may be or has been sold, licensed, or otherwise provided to any third party.

## APPENDIX: STRATEGIC NOTES

**Why FOIA first:**
- FOIA is protected First Amendment activity. You cannot be penalized for filing.
- The data you get back becomes the *evidence* for the ordinance, the lawsuit, and the public testimony.
- Most PDs will try to stall, deny, or overcharge. You appeal. You send the denial to the ACLU/EFF.
- If they claim the data is "investigative records exempt" — the contract, invoices, and policies are NOT exempt. They have to produce those.

**What to do with the FOIA response:**
1. Upload to a public repository (documentcloud.org, GitHub) — name the files by city
2. Send to: press@aclu.org, info@eff.org, and the reporter who covered Flock for your local paper
3. Bring the key findings (how many searches, out-of-state shares) to the first city council meeting

**If they deny everything:**
- File an appeal with the [State Public Access Counselor / Attorney General]
- Contact your local ACLU affiliate — they do FOIA litigation
- Publish the denial letter. It's evidence of secrecy.

**If they say they need 30+ days and want $500:**
- Negotiate the fee down. Cite the public interest fee waiver statute.
- Set a calendar reminder. Follow up on day 31.
- Most PDs sit on FOIAs until sued. Don't be intimidated. The statute is on your side.
